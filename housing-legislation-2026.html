21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644)

H.R. 6644 became law on July 11, 2026, as Public Law 119-101, without the President's signature. The enrolled text is a housing-supply and HUD-program reform law; it does not change LIHTC allocations or amend the Community Reinvestment Act.

Status, re-checked October 11, 2026

H.R. 6644 is enacted law (Public Law 119-101). Congress sent it to the President after final passage on June 23, 2026, and it became law on July 11, 2026 without a signature. As of October 11, 2026 COHO found no HUD notice putting the CDBG new-construction change into effect.

The ROAD Act does not amend the Internal Revenue Code: it does not change Low-Income Housing Tax Credit allocations or basis rules, and it does not enact the Affordable Housing Credit Improvement Act (AHCIA). It does not amend the Community Reinvestment Act. Section 203 raises the limit on public welfare investments by national banks and state member banks from 15 to 20 percent (12 U.S.C. 24 and 12 U.S.C. 338a).

Verified Enacted Housing Provisions

ROAD Act Provisions Relevant to Affordable Housing

CDBG Affordable Housing Construction

Section 204 adds new construction of affordable housing as an eligible Community Development Block Grant activity, capped at 20 percent of a grantee's allocation and applying only to money appropriated after the law took effect.

Relevance: Potential local gap-financing flexibility

HOME Program Reform

Section 501 reauthorizes the HOME Investment Partnerships Program (with no dollar amount; appropriations still set funding) and reforms it, including statutory environmental-review exemptions for new construction infill housing projects and acquisition of real property for affordable housing purposes.

Relevance: Administrative and review-path changes for HOME-assisted housing

Choice in Affordable Housing

Section 405 allows certain units in LIHTC-financed, HOME-assisted, or Rural Housing Service-assisted buildings to satisfy voucher inspection requirements through recent program inspections.

Relevance: Less duplicative inspection friction for eligible assisted units

Manufactured Housing and Homeownership

Section 301 lets manufactured homes be built without a permanent chassis; Section 105 lets FHA start a pilot for mortgages of $100,000 or less, and Sections 401–402 order CFPB studies of small-loan costs; Section 1001 generally bars large institutional investors (those controlling 350 or more single-family homes) from buying more, starting 180 days after enactment and ending 15 years later; it exempts several kinds of purchase, including homes built or substantially renovated to rent, rent-to-own programs, foreclosure and loss-mitigation acquisitions, purchases from other large investors, and 55-plus communities.

Relevance: Supply and homeownership-side policy, not a tax-credit pricing rule

Tax Credit Market Read-Through

The ROAD Act is relevant to LIHTC readers mainly through housing-program administration and potential gap-financing channels. It should not be modeled as a direct Section 42 credit increase.

  • No LIHTC cap change: The ROAD Act does not change LIHTC allocations or eligible basis.
  • Bank investment limit, not a CRA change: Section 203 lets national banks and state member banks raise their ceiling on public welfare investments, a category that includes LIHTC equity, from 15 to 20 percent. The higher limit does not reach state nonmember banks or credit unions. The Community Reinvestment Act itself is unchanged.
  • Potential indirect benefit: CDBG/HOME flexibility and inspection-recognition provisions may affect feasibility for some affordable housing projects, but those effects should be underwritten project by project.

What It Means in Colorado

The Act moves money and rules through programs Colorado already runs. Who feels it depends on whether a town gets HUD money directly or through the state.

  • CDBG for new construction. Colorado's larger cities and counties receive CDBG from HUD directly. Smaller ones, cities under 50,000 that are not principal cities and counties under 200,000, receive it through the state: the Division of Housing (DOH) for housing and the Division of Local Government for public facilities. Either way, the new-construction use applies only to money appropriated after July 11, 2026 and is capped at 20 percent of the grantee's allocation. HUD had published no rule or notice implementing it when this page was checked on October 11, 2026.
  • HOME. DOH runs the state HOME program for the parts of Colorado outside local participating jurisdictions and requires 30 years of affordability. Section 501(i) removes HOME's 24-month commitment deadline, which takes pressure off DOH and local HOME programs when a tax credit deal's closing slips. Section 501(l) also makes four kinds of HOME activity exempt from NEPA review in the statute itself; HUD has a year from enactment to issue any rules it finds necessary.
  • Voucher inspections in tax credit buildings. A Colorado housing authority placing a voucher in a CHFA-financed building can count an inspection from the past 12 months that found the unit suitable for occupancy under the tax credit rules (IRC §42(i)(3)(B)(ii)), if the authority did it or can get its results (Section 405(a)).
  • No change to Colorado's credit supply. Colorado's 2026 federal 9% ceiling, $20,538,908, comes from the per-capita rate in Public Law 119-21, not this Act. CHFA's 2027–28 QAP drafts are written under the same credit law.

Sources: Public Law 119-101 (GovInfo); DOH CDBG housing; DOLA Division of Local Government CDBG; DOH HOME program; IRS Notice 2026-22.

Enactment Timeline

  • February 9, 2026: House passed the original measure.
  • March 12, 2026: Senate passed an amended version under the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act short title.
  • May 20, 2026: House agreed to the Senate amendment with a further amendment.
  • June 22, 2026: Senate concurred in the House amendment with an amendment, 85–5.
  • June 23, 2026: House agreed to the Senate's version, 358–32: final passage.
  • July 11, 2026: H.R. 6644 became law without the President's signature (Public Law 119-101).
