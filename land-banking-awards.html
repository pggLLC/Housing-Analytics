Prop 123 Land Banking Awards

Colorado's voter-approved housing fund (Proposition 123) pays towns, counties and nonprofits to buy land that will later become affordable homes. This map shows every award so far.

Where the awards are

Each award buys a site now so homes can be built there later, at rents or prices that households with low or moderate incomes can afford. Tap a town, or search for one, to see who received the money, what they plan, and where the site stands now. Use the menus to narrow the awards by round, rental or for-sale homes, who holds the award, or status, and to choose what the pin colors show.

    How land banking works

    • Who can get it. Towns, counties and tribal governments receive grants. Nonprofits with a record of providing affordable housing receive loans that are forgiven if they build. Private developers cannot receive it directly.
    • It buys land, not buildings. The land must be zoned, with a development plan, within five years, and the homes permitted and funded within ten. Otherwise the money is repaid.
    • Only places that made a Prop 123 promise qualify. A town or county must first file a commitment to grow its affordable housing with the state Division of Housing. The Division lists who has filed on its commitment filings page.

    About this data

    Home counts are each grantee's estimate in its award list. Land banking buys land, and the homes come later, so these are plans, not homes built.

    Each row on this page is copied from CHFA's award list for its round; open "Row as printed" in the table to see the original. Where a name in a list wraps onto two lines, the page joins it back together. The program is part of the Prop 123 Affordable Housing Financing Fund, which CHFA runs for the Colorado Office of Economic Development and International Trade (OEDIT).
