Green Building in a Colorado Deal

Certifications, electrification, utility allowances and programs

The short answer

  • Every CHFA tax credit deal needs a green certification. You pick Enterprise Green Communities (EGC), LEED or the National Green Building Standard (NGBS). Yearly energy reporting comes with it, and EV-ready parking unless the project is acquisition/rehab or 100% for people experiencing homelessness or Special Populations. It is a threshold, not points.
  • All-electric is not required and earns no points, but CHFA calls it, and higher certifications, “more competitive.”
  • The federal energy credits are mostly gone. 45L ends for homes acquired after June 30, 2026, and 179D for buildings whose construction begins after that date. 48E solar ends for systems in service after 2027 unless construction started early.
  • What pays now is local: a lower utility allowance, the state heat pump credit, Xcel and co-op programs, Energy Outreach Colorado for existing buildings, and CCEF or C-PACE loans.

Which program for which deal

Start with two facts about the site: which edition of the International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) the town enforces, and which utility serves it. Most of the choice follows from those. Each row links to the rule or program it relies on, further down.

Why it pays, for residents and owners

Residents get lower bills and a more comfortable home. Owners get rent: a tax credit rent cap covers rent plus a utility allowance, so when the allowance goes down, the rent the owner can charge goes up.

How it moves a deal

The certification choice sets hard cost, the town's code sets the baseline, and metering sets who pays which bill. No Colorado source publishes a reliable cost premium for each certification, so this page gives none; get numbers from your contractor and green rater at schematic design.

Federal credits: what ended or is at risk

The 2025 federal tax law (P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) ended or narrowed most of the energy credits a pro forma used to count. Don't carry them in a new deal unless your tax counsel confirms the project qualifies.
