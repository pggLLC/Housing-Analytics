# Fruita Commons (Homeownership Portion) — Professional Engagement Scopes

**Type:** procurement scoping — the two professional engagements the screening tool cannot replace.
**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Owner:** Fruita Housing Authority ("the Authority")
**Grounding:** the completed Phase 1–9 screening platform, its accumulated caveats (competitive-supply gap, capture humility, all-null demand assumptions), the land-disposition Model A hypothesis, the deed-restricted failure-mode register, and the Phase-11 validation list. The CoHO screening report is the **companion document to both scopes** — hand it to both firms so the Authority does not pay to reproduce work already done.

**Division of labor (the boundary both scopes must respect):**

| Already produced by the CoHO screening (do not re-buy) | Must come from the consultant | Must come from counsel |

## PART A — Scope of Work: For-Sale Fundamental Market Study

**Engagement:** an independent market-study firm with Colorado attainable/for-sale experience (CHFA-market-study-grade discipline; the Fruita Mews rental studies by Kinetic Valuation Group and Prior & Associates are the local precedent standard).
**Subject:** ~50 for-sale townhomes (4×1BR flex / 22×2BR / 22×3BR / 2×4BR), income-restricted across 70–120% AMI, on Fruita Housing Authority land, with a deed-restriction / shared-equity structure to be finalized.
**Budget/timeline expectation:** $15,000–$40,000; 6–10 weeks.

### Task 1 — Market area delineation
1.1 Delineate the for-sale primary market area using commute and socioeconomic structure (the professional rental PMAs at this exact site — 8 Mesa County tracts reaching western Grand Junction — are the starting reference; the consultant must justify any departure for for-sale product).
1.2 Define the secondary market area and the expected outside-PMA share, reconciled against the documented 9–56% in-migration range observed at Fruita Mews.

### Task 2 — Economic and demographic base
2.1 Employment base and drivers (employers, wage distribution, commute patterns to Grand Junction), with 3–5-year direction.
2.2 Household growth, in-migration, tenure trends, and household-size mix in the PMA — **forward-looking**, not only current-census (the screening intentionally stops at observed data).

### Task 3 — Competitive supply and pipeline  ⟵ *the gap no tool data covers*
3.1 Inventory of existing for-sale supply relevant to the subject: attached and detached, by price band; months of supply below ~$500k.
3.2 **Pipeline census:** platted/under-construction/proposed subdivisions and attached-product projects in the PMA, with unit counts, price positioning, and delivery timing.
3.3 Existing deed-restricted ownership stock and its resale activity (WMRHC and any local program units).
3.4 New-construction townhome pricing and $/sq-ft versus resale; base pricing for the subject's product type (the screening uses all-homes ZHVI $486,295 and Redfin sale $489,439 as placeholders — the consultant must supply product-specific figures).

### Task 4 — Demand analysis (primary research required)
4.1 Income-qualified potential buyer pool by AMI band (70–120%), tenure, and household size — reconciled to the screening's observed pool and its stated CHAS limitation above 100% AMI (consultant must use an above-100% income source, e.g. ACS B19001).
4.2 **Primary research (mandatory, methods stated):** interviews with ≥3 active PMA brokers; ≥2 participating lenders (including USDA RD context) on pre-qualification clearance rates for this buyer profile; HRWC counseling-pipeline data; at least one employer perspective on workforce housing demand.
4.3 First-time-buyer share, down-payment readiness, and credit readiness — evidence-based estimates for exactly the eleven reduction stages the screening leaves null (the report's funnel table is the checklist).
4.4 Buyer acceptance of the deed-restriction / shared-equity / ground-lease structure — survey or structured-interview evidence, not assertion.

### Task 5 — Product and pricing recommendations
5.1 Unit type/size/mix assessment against market expectations (validate or revise the 4/22/22/2 program).
5.2 Recommended restricted price points by AMI band and the restricted-vs-unrestricted value differential (the appraisal-relevant discount).
5.3 HOA level the market will bear, in context of the $175–225 target and comparable HOA burdens.

### Task 6 — Absorption, capture, and sellout
6.1 Supportable monthly sales pace and total sellout period for the subject, by AMI band and unit type, **with every capture denominator stated**.
6.2 Capture rates against the consultant's qualified pool; comparison of 24/30/36/48-month sellout scenarios; expected contract-fallout rate and gross contracts required.
6.3 Calibration against at least two actual comparable absorption histories in Mesa/Garfield/Eagle counties.

### Task 7 — Reconciliation and conclusions
7.1 **Explicit reconciliation against the CoHO screening report:** where the study confirms, refines, or contradicts the screening's ladder, gaps, pool, and scenarios — and why. (This reconciliation is a deliverable section, not a courtesy.)
7.2 Conclusions: is the program marketable as proposed; at what prices, pace, and assistance levels; and what changes would the consultant make.

### Deliverables, qualifications, exclusions
- Written report suitable for lender/funder/council use; the reconciliation section; interview/method appendix; data files for the demand and supply tables.
- Firm must disclose Colorado for-sale and affordable-ownership references; the analyst signing must be identified.
- **Exclusions (already covered or separately procured):** appraisal, subsidy-gap arithmetic, shared-equity lifecycle modeling, legal opinions, environmental/site work.

## PART B — Scope of Work: Legal Counsel (Homeownership Portion)

**Engagement:** Colorado real-estate/affordable-housing counsel with CCIOA, common-interest, deed-restriction, and public-entity experience; construction-defect (CDARA) capability in-house or associated.
**Sequenced in three stages so fees track decisions.**

### Stage 1 — Structure selection and risk posture (before the land decision)
1.1 **Land-disposition structure memo:** legal mechanics, risks, and document requirements for each model under consideration — (A) Authority fee retention with 99-year renewable ground lease; (B) discounted lot conveyance with permanent covenant and recapture lien; (D) master ground lease to a stewardship entity — including public-entity authority (C.R.S. Title 29 housing-authority powers), disposition/procurement requirements, and reversion/termination mechanics. (Model C — full fee sale with deed restriction only — analyzed as the fallback comparison.)
1.2 **Property-tax opinion:** the exemption position while the Authority holds land; taxability of improvements under a ground lease; availability of restricted-value assessment for deed-restricted homes — coordinated with the Mesa County Assessor and delivered as a written position the underwriting can rely on.
1.3 **CDARA / construction-defect posture:** exposure assessment for attached for-sale product; plat-form recommendation (**condominium map vs fee-simple townhome plat**) with defect-liability, insurance, and lender-acceptance consequences; required contractual risk allocation with the builder (warranty terms, notice-and-repair, insurance specifications).
1.4 **Lender-conformity screen:** which restriction/ground-lease forms conform to Fannie Mae (including the CLT ground-lease rider), FHA leasehold requirements, and USDA RD resale-restriction rules — so the structure chosen is financeable before it is drafted.

### Stage 2 — Instrument drafting (after structure selection; before developer agreement)
2.1 **Ground lease** (if Model A/D): 99-year renewable form conforming to the Stage-1 lender screen; ground-rent terms and escalation; improvements ownership; assignment; mortgagee protections; default/cure.
2.2 **Affordability covenant / deed restriction:** codification of the selected resale formula (the tool's convention set — fixed-simple / lesser-of / shared-appreciation — as the menu); term or perpetuity; income-eligibility and owner-occupancy requirements; capital-improvement credits; hardship and estate provisions; **foreclosure-survival architecture** (priority, lender notice-and-cure, Authority/steward purchase option or right of first refusal).
2.3 **Common-interest regime under CCIOA:** declaration, bylaws, HOA formation (or master-lease alternative), budget/reserve requirements, developer-transition provisions, and the interaction between HOA assessments and the affordability covenant (assessment-lien priority).
2.4 **Subordinate financing instruments:** deferred second (HRWC/DOLA/CHFA-conforming) note and deed-of-trust forms; recapture and shared-appreciation provisions; intercreditor/subordination terms consistent with the first-mortgage products identified in Stage 1.
2.5 **Stewardship agreement:** administration contract (candidate: HRWC) covering eligibility certification, occupancy monitoring, resale administration, waitlist management, fee structure, records, replacement/assignment of the steward, and survival if the steward fails.

### Stage 3 — Transaction and consumer-facing documents (before sales)
3.1 Purchase and sale agreement package for restricted homes; **plain-language disclosure** of the resale restriction, ground lease, and shared-equity terms (consumer-protection quality — the buyer must demonstrably understand what they are buying).
3.2 Buyer-selection instruments: lottery/waitlist procedures, any local-preference policy **reviewed for fair-housing compliance (disparate-impact analysis)**, and the affirmative marketing plan review.
3.3 Developer agreement support: disposition/development agreement between the Authority and the selected builder (procurement compliance, performance security, affordability delivery obligations, defect-risk allocation per Stage 1.3).
3.4 Closing-form set and title coordination (title company instruction letter for the restriction/lease recording sequence and lien priority).
3.5 Funding-source compliance overlays as commitments land (Prop 123 / DOLA / CHFA / FHLBank document requirements folded into the instrument set).

### Deliverables and gating
- Stage 1: three memos (structure, tax, CDARA/plat) + lender-conformity matrix — **gates the land-model board decision**.
- Stage 2: execution-ready instrument set — **gates the developer agreement**.
- Stage 3: transaction package — **gates sales launch**.
- Fee structure: fixed-fee per stage preferred; Stage 1 is the small, decision-critical purchase to make first.

## Sequencing the two engagements
1. **Start legal Stage 1 immediately** — the structure/tax/CDARA memos change what the market study should test (plat form affects product; tax treatment affects price points).
2. **Commission the market study once Stage 1 lands** (or concurrently, with the structure memo shared mid-study) — and hand the consultant the CoHO screening report + the scenario fixture on day one.
3. **Feed both back into the tool:** the study's shares resolve the demand funnel; counsel's structure decision selects the land model and resale convention; the screening report regenerates as the underwriting spine both firms reference.

*Both scopes assume the homeownership portion only; any rental or mixed-use component is separately scoped. Nothing here is legal advice — it is a procurement outline for retaining those who give it.*
