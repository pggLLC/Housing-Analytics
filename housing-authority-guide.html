Starting a Housing Authority in Colorado

A Colorado city, town or county can create a housing authority by resolution after 25 residents petition and a public hearing is held. Two or more local governments can create a joint authority by contract instead. Creating one is a few months of public process. Making it build or own housing takes money, staff and a board that can judge real estate, finance and legal documents. An authority without those exists on paper only.

Three ways to form one

RouteWho creates itBoardCan it levy a tax?
City or town authority C.R.S. 29-4-204, 29-4-205 City council or town board Up to 9 commissioners appointed by the mayor, or the council itself No
County authority C.R.S. 29-4-503, 29-4-504 Board of county commissioners Up to 11 commissioners appointed by the board chair, or the board itself No
Joint (multijurisdictional) authority C.R.S. 29-1-204.5 Two or more cities, towns or counties, by contract A board of directors set by the contract Yes, with voter approval: sales or use tax up to 1%, property tax up to 5 mills

Towns are covered by the city route: the statute defines "city" as "any city or incorporated town included in the territorial boundaries of the authority" (C.R.S. 29-4-203).

Steps for a city, town or county authority

  1. Twenty-five residents file a petition. "Any twenty-five residents of the city may file a petition with the city clerk" saying an authority is needed. A county petition goes to the clerk of the board of county commissioners. C.R.S. 29-4-204(1); 29-4-503
  2. The clerk gives notice of a public hearing. Notice runs in a local newspaper at least ten days before the hearing, at the local government's expense. C.R.S. 29-4-204(1); 29-4-503
  3. The hearing is held. "A full opportunity to be heard shall be granted to all residents and taxpayers." C.R.S. 29-4-204(2)
  4. The governing body decides whether there is a need. A city or town asks whether "unsanitary or unsafe inhabited dwelling accommodations exist," or whether safe or sanitary housing is short for its residents; either finding is enough. A county asks whether decent, safe and sanitary housing is short for agricultural workers, their families and other low-income families. If it finds no need, it adopts a resolution denying the petition. A new petition can be filed after three months, and residents can force a public vote with a larger petition (for a county, signatures equal to 5% of the votes cast for governor or president at the last general election). C.R.S. 29-4-204; 29-4-503
  5. It adopts a resolution and appoints commissioners. A city or town may appoint up to nine; a county up to eleven. Either may instead make its own elected members the commissioners. C.R.S. 29-4-205(2), (3)(a); 29-4-504(2), (3)(a)
  6. It files the certificate with the state. A certificate of the appointments is filed with the Division of Local Government in the Colorado Department of Local Affairs. Once filed, the commissioners form a "body corporate and politic." C.R.S. 29-4-503; 29-4-204
  7. The new authority organizes. It adopts bylaws and names officers (C.R.S. 29-4-209(1)(u)). Adopting an annual budget is good practice, though the Local Government Budget Law does not apply to county and municipal housing authorities (C.R.S. 29-1-102(13)). The Local Government Audit Law does: an audit is due to the authority "within seven months after the close of the fiscal year," with a copy to the State Auditor within thirty days, unless the State Auditor approves the authority's application for an exemption because its revenue and spending are small. C.R.S. 29-1-606(1)(c), (3); 29-1-604

Board rules the statute sets

  • Terms: staggered at first, then up to five years for a city or town; five years for a county. C.R.S. 29-4-205(3)(b); 29-4-504(3)(b)
  • Elected officials: on an appointed board, "not more than one of such commissioners may be a county official," and the city rule is the same for city officials. The limit does not apply when the council or county board chooses to sit as the authority itself. C.R.S. 29-4-504(2), (3)(a); 29-4-205(2), (3)(a)
  • A resident seat: at least one commissioner must be someone "directly assisted by the authority." An authority with fewer than 300 public housing units is excused if it gives reasonable notice to its resident advisory board and no eligible person says they want to serve. Federal rules set the same seat and exception for an authority that runs public housing or vouchers. C.R.S. 29-4-205(3.5); 29-4-504(3.5); 24 CFR 964.415
  • No pay: commissioners are reimbursed for "actual and necessary expenses" but are not paid. C.R.S. 29-4-205(4); 29-4-504(4)
  • Conflicts: a commissioner or employee may not hold "any interest, direct or indirect, in any project or in any property included or planned to be included in any project," must "immediately disclose the same in writing to the authority," and failing to disclose "shall constitute misconduct in office." C.R.S. 29-4-207

The statute sets the minimum. The petition, resolution, bylaws and conflict policy are local documents; have the local government's attorney draft them.

Steps for a joint authority

  1. The members negotiate a contract. "Any combination of home rule or statutory cities, towns, counties, and cities and counties of this state may" create a separate governmental entity by contract. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(1)
  2. The contract sets the board and the money. It must establish a governing board of directors, how directors are appointed, their terms and how vacancies are filled, and "the expected sources of revenue of the authority." C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(2)
  3. Any tax goes to the voters. The authority may levy "a sales or use tax, or both, at a rate not to exceed one percent" and a property tax "at a rate not to exceed five mills," but "no action by an authority to establish or increase any tax or development impact fee authorized by this section shall take effect unless first submitted to a vote of the registered electors," at a general election, a biennial local election, or the November election in an odd-numbered year. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(3)(f.1), (3)(f.2), (7.5), as amended by SB 26-001 (effective August 12, 2026)

A Colorado example: the Summit Combined Housing Authority is a multijurisdictional authority "pursuant to Section 29-1-204.5," formed by intergovernmental agreement among Summit County and the Towns of Breckenridge, Dillon, Frisco, Montezuma and others. Town of Breckenridge Resolution 2016-08

What an authority can do

An authority is "a body both corporate and politic, exercising public powers." C.R.S. 29-4-209(1) Among other things it can:

  • "make and execute contracts" and "sue and be sued" 29-4-209(1)(t), (1)(q)
  • "purchase, lease, obtain options upon, or acquire by eminent domain" property, and own, improve and insure it 29-4-209(1)(k), (1)(m)
  • "borrow money upon its bonds, notes, debentures, or other evidences of indebtedness" 29-4-209(1)(o)
  • form one or more corporations under Colorado law, which is how an authority takes an ownership share in a tax credit partnership 29-4-209(2)
  • hold property that "is exempt from all local and municipal taxes" 29-4-227(1)(a)

No new federal public housing. The federal government cannot fund public housing units above the number an authority had on October 1, 1999, so a new authority starts at zero and works with local money, state programs, tax credits and partnerships. Housing Act of 1937, section 9(g)(3); HUD Faircloth Limit guidance

Paper or working: why the board matters

Many Colorado authorities were created by resolution years ago and have no staff, no property and no recent projects. They exist on paper. That is not a failure in itself: a paper authority still holds every power above and can be put to work. But the powers do nothing until someone uses them, and using them means signing contracts, borrowing, buying land and answering to auditors.

In a small authority the commissioners are often the only people who read the deal documents. They approve the land purchase, the partnership agreement with a developer, the loan and bond documents, the budget and the audit, and each one commits a public body. Commissioners are unpaid volunteers (C.R.S. 29-4-205(4)), so the skills on the board are whatever the appointing body recruits.

How the board is builtStrengthTrade-off
Elected officials sit as the board 29-4-205(2); 29-4-504(2) Fast to set up; direct link to local land and fee decisions No more independent than the council; members turn over with elections and seldom have development or finance experience
Appointed residents, no skill criteria Community voice; easy to fill The board may not be able to judge a pro forma, a partnership agreement or a bond deal, which leaves staff or the developer unchecked
Appointed board with named skill seats (recommended for a working authority) Seats for finance or lending, development or construction, law, property management or compliance, and local planning, plus the required resident seat and at most one elected official. The board can read and question what it signs and lasts across elections Harder to recruit unpaid professionals in small towns; members with industry ties must disclose and stay out of any project they have an interest in (29-4-207)

Why named skill seats

The reason is in the statute itself. The authority can borrow, condemn land and form corporations (29-4-209), its books are audited each year unless the State Auditor grants a small-authority exemption (29-1-604, 29-1-606), and an undisclosed interest is misconduct in office (29-4-207). A board that cannot judge those decisions cannot oversee them. The federal housing department makes the same point for authorities that run its programs: its free Lead the Way course exists to help authority "boards and staff fulfill their responsibilities in providing effective governance and oversight." HUD Office of Public and Indian Housing, Lead the Way: PHA Governance and Financial Management

The cost is recruitment. Where a small town cannot fill skill seats, a county or joint authority pools people and staff across a region.

Signs an authority is working

  • Paid staff, or a contract with another authority to run its programs
  • Property it owns, or a partnership it holds a share in
  • A recent project or program
  • An audit, or an approved audit exemption, on file with the State Auditor for the latest year (29-1-604; 29-1-606(3))
  • Board minutes showing it meets

Tax exemptions, the contractor and the subcontractors

An authority's ownership share is what makes a project tax-exempt, so the exemptions follow the authority into its partnerships. There are two: property tax for as long as the authority holds its share, and sales and use tax on construction materials while the project is built.

Which projects qualify

  • The authority itself is "exempt from the payment of any taxes or fees" to the state or local governments, and "all property of an authority is exempt from all local and municipal taxes." C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(a) Sales of goods to a housing authority are exempt from state and state-collected sales tax. C.R.S. 39-26-704; Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects (June 2025)
  • A project owned by a partnership or company in which the authority holds an ownership interest, or one wholly owned by the authority or with the authority as sole member, is exempt from property tax, and from sales and use tax during construction. C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(b)
  • Only the low-income share is exempt. Both exemptions apply in proportion to the share of the project for occupancy by persons of low income. The authority picks square footage or cost to measure that share, and its figure is "presumed valid absent manifest error." C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(b)

Property tax

The exemption is claimed with the county assessor, not the state. The authority or developer sends the assessor a letter or exemption certificate stating the authority's ownership percentage, the statute, and the share of the project that qualifies. It lasts while the authority holds its ownership interest. Rural assessors may not have seen one before, so expect to explain the ownership structure. Alex Gano, "Affordable Housing Tax Exemptions in Colorado," Colorado Lawyer, December 2025 (54 Colo. Law. 32)

Sales and use tax on construction: step by step

  1. The authority certifies the project. It issues a statement of its ownership interest, certifies the low-income percentage, and sets "the beginning and ending dates for the construction." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects
  2. The owner gets its exempt number. The qualifying entity applies on the Application for Exempt Entity Certificate (DR 0715). Its Colorado exemption number begins with "98." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; DR 0172 instructions
  3. The general contractor applies for the project certificate. It files the Contractor Application for Exemption Certificate (DR 0172), one per project. The application attaches the contract signed by the exempt owner, showing the scope of work, the bid amount ("to the penny," matching the application), the owner's 98 number, and start and completion dates the contract supports. If there is a warranty period, its end is the completion date. Existing contractors with a federal employer number can file online in Revenue Online; first-time applicants and extension requests go by email. Incomplete applications are denied. DR 0172 (revised 10/10/24)
  4. The general contractor issues copies to each subcontractor. "Subcontractors will not be issued Certificates of Exemption by the Department of Revenue." The prime contractor makes a copy for each subcontractor, fills in that subcontractor's name and address, and signs it. The prime keeps the original, and keeps copies of every certificate it issued to subcontractors at its place of business "for a minimum of three years" for audit. DR 0172, Special Notice
  5. Everyone shows the certificate at the time of purchase. Without it, the supplier must charge tax; the supplier may, but need not, credit the tax later. Department of Revenue General Information Letter GIL-15-002 (2015); C.R.S. 39-26-708
  6. Buy only what qualifies. Building materials, fixtures and appliances used during construction qualify. Tools and equipment the contractor keeps, rentals, supplies that do not become part of the building, and routine maintenance after completion do not. "Any unauthorized use of the exemption certificate will result in revocation." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; DR 0172
  7. Mixed-use or mixed-income projects pay tax on the rest. The exemption is partial, and the qualifying entity remits tax on the non-exempt share on a sales tax return (DR 0100), at least quarterly. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects
  8. Tax already paid can be refunded to the owner. The qualifying entity, not the contractor, claims it on the Claim for Refund of Tax Paid to Vendors (DR 0137B), with the authority's statement and the receipts or invoices. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects
  9. If the schedule slips, extend the certificate. One extension can be requested by email or phone; more, or longer than six months, need supporting documents. DR 0172 instructions

Home-rule cities run their own sales tax

The state process above covers state sales tax and the city, county and special district taxes the Department of Revenue collects. It "does not apply to sales taxes administered directly by home rule cities." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects Denver and Colorado Springs have taken the position that their own sales and use tax is a local matter the state exemption does not override. Before bidding, ask the city whether it will honor the exemption. If it will, get that in a resolution of its governing body; if it will not, carry the city's sales and use tax in the construction budget. Gano, Colorado Lawyer, December 2025

Whether a given partnership qualifies, how the low-income share is measured, and what a home-rule city will honor all depend on the deal. Have the authority's counsel and the project's tax adviser confirm them before the contractor applies.
