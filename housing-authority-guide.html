Starting a Housing Authority in Colorado

A Colorado city, town or county can create a housing authority by resolution after 25 residents petition and a public hearing is held. Two or more local governments can create a joint authority by contract instead. Creating one is a few months of public process. Making it build or own housing takes money, staff and a board that can judge real estate, finance and legal documents. An authority without those exists on paper only.

The legal points in brief

  • Who creates it: a city or town council or a board of county commissioners, by resolution after a resident petition and a public hearing; or two or more cities, towns, counties or cities and counties, by contract. C.R.S. 29-4-204; 29-4-503; 29-1-204.5(1)
  • Taxing power: only a joint authority can levy a tax, and only with voter approval. A city, town or county authority cannot. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(3)(f.1), (3)(f.2), (7.5)
  • City and county commissioners: unpaid, barred from any interest in the authority's projects or contracts, and required to disclose in writing any property they already own that a project includes. C.R.S. 29-4-205(4); 29-4-207; 29-4-504(4), (7)
  • Annual duties: an audit, or an approved small-authority exemption, each year, unless the authority's finances are fully reported in the audit of the city, county or member governments; a joint authority also adopts and files a budget under state budget law, unless its finances are fully reported in a member government's budget. Details are in the steps below. C.R.S. 29-1-603(1); 29-1-604; 29-1-606; 29-1-103(1); 29-1-204.5(2)(b)(IV)
  • Tax exemptions: the same for city, county and joint authorities. The authority's own property and purchases are exempt; a project held through a partnership or company with an authority interest is exempt from property tax, and from sales and use tax during construction, for its low-income share. C.R.S. 29-4-227; 29-4-507; 29-1-204.5(10)
  • Who confirms it for a deal: the local government's attorney drafts the formation documents; the authority's counsel and the project's tax adviser confirm the exemptions before the contractor applies.

Three ways to form one

RouteWho creates itBoardCan it levy a tax?
City or town authority C.R.S. 29-4-204, 29-4-205 City council or town board Up to 9 commissioners appointed by the mayor (or the appointing authority the charter or an ordinance names) and confirmed by the council, or the council itself; exactly 9 at first in a city and county over 300,000 No
County authority C.R.S. 29-4-503, 29-4-504 Board of county commissioners Up to 11 commissioners appointed by the board chair, or the board itself No
Joint (multijurisdictional) authority C.R.S. 29-1-204.5 Two or more cities, towns or counties, by contract A board of directors set by the contract Yes, with voter approval: sales or use tax up to 1%, property tax up to 5 mills

A county authority does not reach into cities by default. Its boundaries are the county's, but they do not include any city unless that city's governing body passes a resolution to be included, nor any area already inside a city housing authority formed earlier. C.R.S. 29-4-508

Towns are covered by the city route: the statute defines "city" as "any city or incorporated town included in the territorial boundaries of the authority" (C.R.S. 29-4-203).

A fourth, narrower route exists: any city or town may create a veterans' housing authority by ordinance, setting its duties and powers and giving veterans preference in its housing. That route skips the petition and hearing below, but it is a different body from a general housing authority, and this guide covers the three routes above. C.R.S. 29-4-401; 29-4-402

Steps for a city, town or county authority

  1. Twenty-five residents file a petition. "Any twenty-five residents of the city may file a petition with the city clerk" saying an authority is needed. A county petition goes to the clerk of the board of county commissioners. C.R.S. 29-4-204(1); 29-4-503
  2. The clerk gives notice of a public hearing. Notice runs in a local newspaper at least ten days before the hearing, at the local government's expense. Where no newspaper of general circulation exists, the notice is posted in at least three public places, also at least ten days before the hearing. C.R.S. 29-4-204(1); 29-4-503
  3. The hearing is held. "A full opportunity to be heard shall be granted to all residents and taxpayers" of the city, town or county "and to all other interested persons." C.R.S. 29-4-204(2); 29-4-503(2)
  4. The governing body decides whether there is a need. A city or town asks whether "unsanitary or unsafe inhabited dwelling accommodations exist," or whether safe or sanitary housing is short for its residents; either finding is enough. A county asks whether decent, safe and sanitary housing is short for agricultural workers, their families and other low-income families. If it finds no need, it adopts a resolution denying the petition. A new petition can be filed after three months, and residents can force a public vote with a larger petition: signatures of registered electors equal to 5% of the votes cast for governor or president at the last general election, after which a city or town council must call a special election, and a county puts the question on the ballot at the next general election. C.R.S. 29-4-204(6), (8); 29-4-503(3), (5)
  5. It adopts a resolution and chooses the board. The council or county board can make its own members the commissioners, ex officio. Or it can call for a separate board: in a city or town, up to nine commissioners appointed by the mayor or the appointing authority set by charter or ordinance; in a county, up to eleven appointed by the chair of the board of county commissioners. A city or town's appointments, including later ones and vacancies, need council confirmation unless the council has changed the appointment method by resolution after notice and a hearing. A city and county of more than 300,000 people must start with exactly nine. C.R.S. 29-4-204(4); 29-4-205(2), (3)(a), (3)(c), (6); 29-4-504(2), (3)(a)
  6. Two filings make it official. On an appointed board, the mayor (or other appointing authority) or the county board chair files a certificate of each commissioner's appointment with the city clerk or the county clerk and recorder. Separately, the commissioners sign a certificate stating that notice was given, the hearing was held, the need was found and the commissioners were appointed, and file it with the Division of Local Government in the Colorado Department of Local Affairs. Once that is filed, the commissioners form a "body corporate and politic." C.R.S. 29-4-204(4); 29-4-205(3)(b); 29-4-503(2); 29-4-504(3)(b)
  7. The new authority organizes. It adopts bylaws and names officers (C.R.S. 29-4-209(1)(u)). Adopting an annual budget is good practice, though the Local Government Budget Law does not apply to county and municipal housing authorities (C.R.S. 29-1-102(13)). The Local Government Audit Law does: an audit is due to the authority "within seven months after the close of the fiscal year," with a copy to the State Auditor within thirty days after the authority receives it, unless the State Auditor approves the authority's application for an exemption because its revenue and spending are each no more than $1 million (for fiscal years starting in 2025 or later). That application is due within three months after the fiscal year closes; missing it loses the exemption for that year and the next. No separate audit is needed if the authority's finances are fully reported in the city or county's own audit. C.R.S. 29-1-606(1)(c), (3); 29-1-604; 29-1-603(1); 29-1-602(5)(b) A city or town authority must also file a report of the past year's activities with the mayor at least once a year. C.R.S. 29-4-228

Board rules the statute sets

  • Terms (appointed boards): staggered at first, then up to five years for a city or town; five years for a county. When the council or county board sits as the authority, members serve for their elected terms. C.R.S. 29-4-205(2), (3)(b); 29-4-504(2), (3)(b)
  • City and county officials: on an appointed board, "not more than one of such commissioners may be a county official," and the city rule is the same for city officials. The limit does not apply when the council or county board chooses to sit as the authority itself. C.R.S. 29-4-504(2), (3)(a); 29-4-205(2), (3)(a)
  • A resident seat (appointed boards): at least one appointed commissioner must be someone "directly assisted by the authority." An authority with fewer than 300 public housing units is excused if it gives reasonable notice to its resident advisory board and no eligible person says they want to serve. Federal rules set the same seat for an authority that runs public housing or vouchers. The federal exception, which also covers voucher-only authorities, holds only if the authority gives the resident advisory board notice, allows at least 30 days for a resident to come forward, and repeats the notice at least once every year. C.R.S. 29-4-205(3.5); 29-4-504(3.5); 24 CFR 964.415, 964.425
  • No pay: commissioners are reimbursed for "actual and necessary expenses" but are not paid. C.R.S. 29-4-205(4); 29-4-504(4)
  • Conflicts: a commissioner or employee may not "acquire any interest, direct or indirect, in any project or in any property included or planned to be included in any project," and may not have any interest "in any contract or proposed contract for materials or services" for a project. Disclosure does not cure those. Property a commissioner or employee already owns or controls is different: it must be disclosed "immediately" in writing and entered in the minutes, and failing to disclose "shall constitute misconduct in office." On a county authority, the person then may not take part in the authority's action to buy that property or make that contract. C.R.S. 29-4-207; 29-4-504(7)

The statute sets the minimum. The petition, resolution, bylaws and conflict policy are local documents; have the local government's attorney draft them.

Steps for a joint authority

  1. The members negotiate a contract. "Any combination of home rule or statutory cities, towns, counties, and cities and counties of this state may" create a separate governmental entity by contract. The contract takes effect only when each member's governing body, or another body with power to approve it, has approved it, so every council and board votes on the final contract before it is signed. C.R.S. 29-1-203(1); 29-1-204.5(1)
  2. The contract sets the board and the money. Among the terms it must specify: the authority's name, purpose and functions; its boundaries; a board of directors, with how directors are appointed, their terms and how vacancies are filled; how property and assets are divided; the term of the contract and how it can end; and "the expected sources of revenue of the authority." Counsel should check the draft against the full list in the statute. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(2)
  3. It adopts and files a budget each year. Unlike a city or county authority, a joint authority formed by contract falls under the Local Government Budget Law, and the contract must bind its board to that law. Each year the proposed budget goes through a public process: a notice of the hearing, published once in a local newspaper (or, if the proposed budget is $50,000 or less, posted in three public places instead), that says where the public can inspect the budget and that any elector may file objections before adoption, then a hearing where those objections are considered, then a majority vote of the board and a resolution adopting the budget and making appropriations. The adopted budget is filed with the Division of Local Government within 30 days after the fiscal year begins. The Local Government Audit Law also applies: an annual audit, due to the authority within seven months after the fiscal year closes and sent to the State Auditor within thirty days after the authority receives it, unless the State Auditor approves an exemption for a year in which revenue and spending are each no more than $1 million (for fiscal years starting in 2025 or later). The exemption application is due within three months after the fiscal year closes, and the audit is still required if the application is late or denied. Neither a separate budget nor a separate audit is needed if the authority's finances are fully reported in a member government's budget and audit. C.R.S. 29-1-102(13); 29-1-103(1); 29-1-106; 29-1-107; 29-1-108; 29-1-113(1); 29-1-204.5(2)(b)(IV); 29-1-603(1); 29-1-604; 29-1-606
  4. Any tax or debt goes to the voters. The authority may levy "a sales or use tax, or both, at a rate not to exceed one percent" and a property tax "at a rate not to exceed five mills," but "no action by an authority to establish or increase any tax or development impact fee authorized by this section shall take effect unless first submitted to a vote of the registered electors," at a general election, a biennial local election, or the November election in an odd-numbered year. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(3)(f.1), (3)(f.2), (7.5), as amended by SB 26-001 (effective August 12, 2026) The same vote is required before the authority takes on multiple-year debt or another financial obligation that the state constitution's TABOR amendment covers, except obligations of an enterprise, such as one the authority sets up under its enterprise power. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(7.5)(b), (7.5)(c); Colo. Const. art. X, sec. 20(4)(b) A majority of those voting must approve. The county clerk helps run the election and the authority pays the county's costs, and no authority money may be used to urge or oppose the measure. The contract can make the authority itself depend on each member's voters approving it, and that question can share a ballot with the tax or debt question. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(7.5)(c), (7.5)(d) Before any tax is levied, the contract must also state any requirement that member governments consent, and must require a board resolution finding that the tax "will fairly distribute the costs" and "will not impose an undue burden on any particular group," and, for a sales or use tax, a liaison who coordinates collection with the Department of Revenue. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(2)(e)

A Colorado example: the Summit Combined Housing Authority is a multijurisdictional authority "pursuant to Section 29-1-204.5," formed by intergovernmental agreement among Summit County and the Towns of Breckenridge, Dillon, Frisco, Montezuma and others. Town of Breckenridge Resolution 2016-08

What an authority can do

A city, town or county authority is "a body both corporate and politic, exercising public powers." C.R.S. 29-4-209(1) Among other things it can:

  • "make and execute contracts" and "sue and be sued" 29-4-209(1)(t), (1)(q)
  • "purchase, lease, obtain options upon, or acquire by eminent domain" property, and own, improve and insure it 29-4-209(1)(k), (1)(m)
  • "borrow money upon its bonds, notes, debentures, or other evidences of indebtedness" 29-4-209(1)(o)
  • form one or more corporations under Colorado law, which is how an authority takes an ownership share in a tax credit partnership 29-4-209(2)
  • hold property that "is exempt from all local and municipal taxes" 29-4-227(1)(a)

A joint authority draws its powers from its own statute instead: to build and run housing for low and moderate income families and for local employees, contract, borrow, own and lease property, condemn property (but not property owned by a government or by a public utility and devoted to public use), sue and be sued, and set rents and fees. Any other power, such as forming a subsidiary corporation, must be "essential" to its functions and "specified in the contract." It is exempt from taxes and special assessments "on the same basis and subject to the same conditions" as a city authority. C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(3)(a)-(n), (10)

Counsel drafting a joint authority contract should list every power the authority will need for its deals.

Public notice before each project. Before a city, town or county authority starts building or acquires a project, or an addition to one, it must post a notice describing the project with the city or county clerk and publish it once a week for three weeks in a local newspaper. If, within 30 days after both the posting and the last newspaper notice, registered electors equal to 5% of the votes cast for governor or president at the last general election (3% where the population is over 300,000) petition for a vote, the project waits until a majority approves it. A project with a federal assistance contract is outside this process entirely. Preliminary surveys and planning, the reconstruction, replacement, restoration or remodeling of a project that has deteriorated or been damaged or destroyed, and service buildings never need the vote. C.R.S. 29-4-601; 29-4-602

Whether part 6 applies to a particular project, including one held through a partnership, is a question for the authority's counsel before land is bought.

No federal operating money for new public housing. HUD cannot give an authority Capital Fund or Operating Fund allocations for public housing units above the number it had on October 1, 1999 (its Faircloth limit). An authority may still build or buy units above that limit, but they earn no new allocations, so a new authority, whose limit is zero, works with local money, state programs, tax credits and partnerships. There are two ways to get a limit. One is a HUD-approved transfer or consolidation, in which an authority takes over another agency's public housing, with its assets, liabilities and Faircloth limit. The other is the Partnerships Provision of HUD's Rental Assistance Demonstration (RAD), through which another agency can transfer its Faircloth limit; HUD asks agencies interested in that route to contact the RAD Resource Desk. Housing Act of 1937, section 9(g)(3); HUD Faircloth Limit FAQs (June 2020)

Paper or working: why the board matters

Many Colorado authorities were created by resolution years ago and have no staff, no property and no recent projects. They exist on paper. That is not a failure in itself: a paper authority still holds every power above and can be put to work. But the powers do nothing until someone uses them, and using them means signing contracts, borrowing, buying land and answering to auditors.

In a small authority the commissioners are often the only people who read the deal documents. They approve the land purchase, the partnership agreement with a developer, the loan and bond documents, the budget and the audit, and each one commits a public body. City and county commissioners are unpaid volunteers (C.R.S. 29-4-205(4)); a joint authority's directors are paid only if its contract says so (C.R.S. 29-1-204.5(2)(b)(I)). Either way, the skills on the board are whatever the appointing body recruits.

How the board is builtStrengthTrade-off
Elected officials sit as the board 29-4-205(2); 29-4-504(2) Fast to set up; direct link to local land and fee decisions No more independent than the council; members turn over with elections and seldom have development or finance experience
Appointed residents, no skill criteria Community voice; easy to fill The board may not be able to judge a pro forma, a partnership agreement or a bond deal, which leaves staff or the developer unchecked
Appointed board with named skill seats (recommended for a working authority) Seats for finance or lending, development or construction, real estate law, property management or compliance, and accounting, plus, on an appointed city or county board, the required resident seat and at most one city or county official (a joint authority's contract sets its own board). The board can read and question what it signs and lasts across elections Harder to recruit unpaid professionals in small towns; members with industry ties may not take an interest in a project or its contracts, and land they already own that a project needs must be disclosed in writing (29-4-207; 29-4-504(7))

A Colorado example: Colorado Springs recruits its housing authority commissioners for "skills and expertise in the following areas: finance, architecture, project development, real estate and property management," because "backgrounds in these professions allow the board to provide the oversight required for sound governance." City of Colorado Springs, news release, March 6, 2025 The federal housing department makes the same point for authorities that run its programs: its free Lead the Way course exists to help authority "boards and staff fulfill their responsibilities in providing effective governance and oversight." HUD Office of Public and Indian Housing, Lead the Way: PHA Governance and Financial Management

Joint authorities usually seat governments, not skills. Summit Combined Housing Authority has "one director for each Member," chosen by that member's governing body. Town of Breckenridge Resolution 2016-08, agreement section 6(b) Chaffee Housing Authority was set up with three directors appointed by the county, two each by Salida and Buena Vista, and two at large. Ark Valley Voice, October 12, 2020 (news report; the agreement itself was not checked) A joint authority's contract can name skill seats or criteria for any of those directors, so write them in when the contract is drafted. (29-1-204.5(2))

The cost is recruitment. Unpaid professionals are hard to find in a small town, and a commissioner with industry ties may not take any interest in a project or its contracts (29-4-207; 29-4-504(7)). Where a town cannot fill the seats, a county or joint authority can pool people and staff across a region.

Keep the owner apart from the reviewer

Once an authority is working, it is the applicant. It buys the site, signs with the developer, and asks the town for zoning, a plat, fee waivers and building permits. The town's planning commission, development review staff and council then review that same project. When the same people sit on both sides, the town is reviewing its own deal.

  • Ethics law won't separate the roles for you. Colorado's conflict rules for council and planning members reach a "personal or private interest," a "substantial financial interest," or serving a business "as counsel, consultant, representative, or agent." (C.R.S. 24-18-109(2)(b), (3)(a); 31-4-404(2)) An authority seat held as part of a public office is none of those by itself, so a council sitting as the authority will usually still vote on the authority's land-use application. A member who also works for the developer or the tax credit partnership is a different case and may have to step aside. Have the town attorney confirm both for your town.
  • Neighbors and applicants are owed an impartial hearing. A land-use decision on a specific site is quasi-judicial, and "applicants are entitled to an impartial panel"; prejudging a matter is one of the ways decision-makers lose immunity. Colorado Division of Local Government, "Due Process, Ex Parte Contacts, Conflicts of Interest, and Personal Liability"; Cherry Hills Resort Development Co. v. City of Cherry Hills Village, 757 P.2d 622 (Colo. 1988) Courts presume quasi-judicial officials act with "integrity, honesty, and impartiality," and a challenger must prove actual bias, so a challenge may well fail. No Laporte Gravel Corp. v. Board of County Commissioners, 2022 COA 6, ¶¶ 44, 62 A failed challenge can still cost a deal months, and tax credit deadlines don't move.
  • Staff review is the same problem on a smaller scale. If the planner who writes the staff report also manages the authority's project, no one independent has checked it.

What a town can do:

  1. Appoint a separate board (29-4-205(3)(a); 29-4-504(3)(a)). This is the cleanest fix, and the board stays in place when councils turn over.
  2. If the council sits as the board, keep the roles visibly apart. Hold authority meetings on their own agenda with their own minutes, state the dual role on the record at every land-use hearing on an authority project, have the planning commission make the recommendation, and keep authority commissioners off the planning commission.
  3. Separate the staff. The authority's project manager or consultant does not write the planning review; a town with one planner can hire an outside reviewer.
  4. Adopt a written conflict policy that goes beyond the statute's minimum, drafted by the town or county attorney.

Signs an authority is working

  • Paid staff, or a contract with another authority to run its programs
  • Property it owns, or a partnership it holds a share in
  • A recent project or program
  • An audit, or an approved audit exemption, on file with the State Auditor for the latest year, or its finances reported in a parent government's audit (29-1-603(1); 29-1-604; 29-1-606(3))
  • Board minutes showing it meets

Tax exemptions, the contractor and the subcontractors

An authority's ownership share is what makes a partnership project tax-exempt, so the exemptions follow the authority into its partnerships. The two main ones are property tax for as long as the authority holds its share, and sales and use tax on construction materials while the project is built. A private owner that only leases property to the authority gets a narrower exemption: the leased property and the lease income are exempt from tax, but the statute's construction sales and use tax exemption is tied to an entity the authority holds an interest in. C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(a), (1)(b) A third covers special assessments: the authority, its property, property leased to it, and the low-income, non-commercial part of a project owned by or leased to an entity in which it, or a subsidiary it wholly owns, holds an interest are exempt. The one exception is voluntary: an authority or project owner may join the Colorado New Energy Improvement District (C-PACE financing) and accept its special assessment. C.R.S. 29-4-226(1), (2); 29-4-227 These sections are written for city authorities, and the county and joint authority statutes apply them "on the same basis and subject to the same conditions," so everything below holds for all three kinds. C.R.S. 29-4-507; 29-1-204.5(10)

Which projects qualify

  • The authority itself is "exempt from the payment of any taxes or fees" to the state or local governments, and "all property of an authority is exempt from all local and municipal taxes." C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(a) Property leased to an authority for a project is also exempt, as is the lessor's income from the lease. C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(a) Sales of goods to a housing authority are exempt from state and state-collected sales tax. C.R.S. 39-26-704; Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects (June 2025)
  • A project owned by, leased to, or under construction by a partnership or company in which the authority holds an ownership interest, including an interest held through a subsidiary the authority wholly owns or is the sole member of, is exempt from property tax, and from sales and use tax during construction, provided the development is a housing "project" under the statute: housing on terms "within the means of persons of low income," the clearing or repair of unsafe housing, or housing offered without regard to income where the authority determines the project "substantially benefits persons of low income." C.R.S. 29-4-203(12); 29-4-227(1)(b)
  • For those partnership and company projects, only the low-income share is exempt. The authority's own property and purchases are fully exempt. For a project held through a partnership or company, both exemptions apply in proportion to the share of the project for occupancy by persons of low income. The authority picks square footage or cost to measure that share, and its figure is "presumed valid absent manifest error." C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(b)

Property tax

The exemption is claimed with the county assessor, not the state. The authority or developer sends the assessor a letter or exemption certificate stating the ownership percentage held by the authority or its wholly owned subsidiary, the statute, and the share of the project that qualifies. For an ownership structure, it lasts while the authority or that subsidiary holds its interest; property leased to the authority is exempt for the lease instead, so the submission describes the lease rather than an ownership percentage (C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(a)). Rural assessors may not have seen one before, so expect to explain the ownership structure. Alex Gano, "Affordable Housing Tax Exemptions in Colorado," Colorado Lawyer, December 2025 (54 Colo. Law. 32)

Sales and use tax on construction: step by step

  1. The authority certifies the project. It issues a statement of its ownership interest, certifies the low-income percentage, and sets "the beginning and ending dates for the construction." "All applications must be accompanied by" this statement, so a copy goes with both the DR 0715 and the DR 0172. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects
  2. The qualifying entity gets its exempt number. The qualifying entity (the authority, or the entity that owns, leases or is building the project) applies on the Application for Exempt Entity Certificate (DR 0715). Its Colorado exemption number begins with "98." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; DR 0172 instructions The Department defines a qualifying entity as one "wholly or partially owned by" a housing authority, an entity the authority wholly owns, or an entity of which the authority is the sole member, so a tax credit partnership in which the authority or its subsidiary holds only a share qualifies. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; C.R.S. 29-4-227(1)(b)
  3. The general contractor applies for the project certificate. It files the Contractor Application for Exemption Certificate (DR 0172), one per project. The application attaches the housing authority's statement and the contract signed by the contracting parties, including the exempt organization, showing the type and scope of work, the bid amount ("to the penny," matching the application), and start and estimated completion dates. The exempt organization's 98 number goes on the application itself, and an application without it is rejected. If there is a warranty period, its end is the completion date. Existing contractors with a federal employer number can file online in Revenue Online; first-time applicants and extension requests go by email. Incomplete applications are denied. DR 0172 (revised 10/10/24)
  4. The general contractor issues copies to each subcontractor. "Subcontractors will not be issued Certificates of Exemption by the Department of Revenue." The prime contractor makes a copy for each subcontractor, fills in that subcontractor's name and address, and signs it. The prime keeps the original, and keeps copies of every certificate it issued to subcontractors at its place of business "for a minimum of three years" for audit. DR 0172, Special Notice
  5. Everyone shows the certificate at the time of purchase. Without it, the supplier must charge tax; the supplier may, but need not, credit the tax later. Department of Revenue General Information Letter GIL-15-002 (2015); C.R.S. 39-26-708
  6. Buy only what qualifies. Building materials, fixtures and appliances used during construction qualify. Tools and equipment the contractor keeps, rentals, supplies that do not become part of the building, and routine maintenance after completion do not. "Any unauthorized use of the exemption certificate will result in revocation." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; DR 0172
  7. Mixed-use or mixed-income projects pay tax on the rest. The exemption is partial, and the qualifying entity first opens a sales tax account (Colorado Sales Tax and Withholding Account Application, CR 0100), then remits tax on the non-exempt share on a sales tax return (DR 0100), at least quarterly. If its total tax due for the year is under $5,000, it may ask the Department for approval to file once a year. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects
  8. Tax already paid can be refunded to the qualifying entity. The qualifying entity that owns, leases or is building the project, not the contractor, claims it on the Claim for Refund of Tax Paid to Vendors (DR 0137B), with the authority's statement and the receipts or invoices. Claims fall under the general sales tax refund deadline. Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects; C.R.S. 29-4-227(2); 39-26-703(2)(d)
  9. If the schedule slips, extend the certificate. One extension can be requested by email or phone; more, or longer than six months, need supporting documents. DR 0172 instructions

Home-rule cities run their own sales tax

The state process above covers state sales tax and the city, county and special district taxes the Department of Revenue collects. It "does not apply to sales taxes administered directly by home rule cities." Department of Revenue, Affordable Housing Projects Denver and Colorado Springs have taken the position that their own sales and use tax is a local matter the state exemption does not override. Before bidding, ask the city whether it will honor the exemption. If it will, get that in a resolution of its governing body; if it will not, carry the city's sales and use tax in the construction budget. Gano, Colorado Lawyer, December 2025

Whether a given partnership qualifies, how the low-income share is measured, and what a home-rule city will honor all depend on the deal. Have the authority's counsel and the project's tax adviser confirm them before the contractor applies.
