# Colorado Housing Week: Nov 2 – 8, 2026

*Sunday edition, prepared Sun Nov 8, 2026. Every item links to its source; the date in brackets is the source's publish date. Times are Mountain Time. Election counts are unofficial until counties certify (by Nov 25) and the Secretary of State certifies (by Nov 30).*

## The short version

1. **The southwest Colorado floods hit twice, and about 700 homes are damaged.** The San Juan crested again on Nov 1, higher than on Oct 23. All ten members of Colorado's delegation signed a letter backing the governor's request for a federal disaster declaration. That request still covers public infrastructure only, not help for households.
2. **The election made state housing money a bit safer.** Prop NN passed, and Amendment 87 (graduated income tax) is ahead with about 51% after its backers declared victory. Democrats will hold veto-proof majorities in both chambers when the session opens Jan 11.
3. **HUD opened an "Express Lane" for FHA loans on new LIHTC deals.** It covers 221(d)(4) new-construction loans on 9% and 4% bond deals for experienced teams. It took effect Nov 2.
4. **Polis sent his last budget request, with no plan yet for a $1.6 billion gap.** He presents it to the Joint Budget Committee on Nov 12. Housing lines were not in the coverage, so watch that hearing.
5. **Rates eased for a third week, to 7.18%.** In metro Denver, 65% of October sales involved seller concessions.

## Prop 123 and local commitments

**The Nov 1 commitment deadline has passed, and DOLA hasn't posted the list yet.** We found no updated list of jurisdictions that filed 2027–2029 commitments this week. DOLA's last release, on Oct 15, counted 11,249 approved units submitted across 41 jurisdictions, including 3,405 in Denver. To be fully compliant, a jurisdiction must also meet its goal (or get a waiver) and have a fast-track policy in effect by **Dec 31, 2026**.
*Why it matters:* check a site's jurisdiction against DOLA's filed list before you count on Prop 123 eligibility in a 2027 application.
Source: [DOLA release](https://cdola.colorado.gov/press-release/dola-announces-major-statewide-milestone-in-proposition-123-affordable-housing-goals) [Oct 15, 2026]

**Prop NN passed.** The Governor's Office announced its approval on Nov 5. NN keeps about $500 million a year that would otherwise go back as TABOR refunds and spends it on K-12. It also rewrites the clause that lets the legislature cut Prop 123 money in a tight year. Under the new wording, a cut is allowed only if revenue falls below the TABOR limit by more than the education amount.
*Why it matters:* this gives Prop 123 some protection in statute during a year with a $1.6 billion shortfall. It is our reading of the Blue Book text and has not been checked by counsel.
Sources: [Governor's Office](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/governor-polis-celebrates-approval-prop-nn-and-7a-ballot-measure) [Nov 5, 2026]; [2026 Blue Book, p. 87](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2026-blue-book-en-accessible.pdf)

## Local actions and ballot results

**Amendment 87 (graduated income tax) is ahead, but not yet formally called.** On Friday morning it had 51% yes and a lead of about 71,000 votes. Backers declared victory on Nov 5. It would raise about $2.7 billion more for schools, health care and early childhood. None of that money goes to housing, and home sales keep the 4.4% rate.
*Why it matters:* the new revenue would ease pressure on the general fund, which makes it less likely the legislature reaches for housing money to fill the gap.
Source: [Colorado Politics](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/06/colorado-passes-graduated-income-tax-eliminates-flat-rate/) [Nov 6, 2026]

**Veto-proof Democratic majorities at the Capitol.** Democrats will hold "at least 24 seats in the Senate and 44 in the House" when the session opens **Jan 11, 2027**. House Democrats chose Rep. Jennifer Bacon of Denver as the next Speaker on Nov 6.
*Why it matters:* the 2024 land-use laws (ADUs, transit areas, parking) and the new Prop 123 targets have a bigger majority behind them now. Local resistance is growing too. Cherry Hills Village voters wrote local-only zoning control into their charter this week.
Sources: [Colorado Politics](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/04/5-takeaways-as-democrats-cement-supermajorities-in-general-assembly/) [Nov 4, 2026]; [Colorado Newsline via States Newsroom](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/rep-jennifer-bacon-%E2%80%8Bbecomes-first-black-woman%E2%80%8B-elected-%E2%80%8Bcolorado-house-speaker) [Nov 6, 2026]

**Boulder passes a tax on vacant homes.** The city confirmed on Nov 4 that voters approved a vacancy excise tax of "roughly $4,000 a year for each vacant property." A home counts as vacant if it sits empty for more than 183 days a year. The money goes to general city services, not housing. Council will adopt the ordinance in 2027, it takes effect Jan 1, 2028, and 2028 is the first full year of collection. Voters turned down the city's recreation and public safety bond.
*Why it matters:* resort and Front Range towns will be watching how Boulder defines and enforces "vacant." This tax doesn't fund housing, so COHO's ballot tracker doesn't list it.
Source: [City of Boulder](https://bouldercolorado.gov/news/city-boulder-voters-support-three-ballot-measures-nov-3-2026-election) [Nov 4, 2026]

**Englewood's only overnight shelter has closed.** Bridge House closed its Tri-Cities Homeless Navigation Center in Englewood, blaming the "unpredictability of the current funding landscape." Its Englewood Ready to Work program closes in mid-November, and its Boulder program closes by Dec 31. Some residents got only a few days' notice. The Englewood, Littleton and Sheridan councils hold a joint study session on the center's future on **Nov 12 at 6 p.m.**
*Why it matters:* the south metro area loses shelter beds just as winter starts. Supportive and transitional housing projects in Arapahoe County may now be asked to take on more.
Source: [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/in-your-community/south-metro/englewoods-only-overnight-homeless-shelter-closes-bridge-house-to-wind-down-all-operations) [Nov 5, 2026]

## Disasters and housing loss

**Southwest Colorado: a second, higher flood, and about 700 homes damaged.** The San Juan River at Pagosa Springs crested near 15 feet on Oct 23 and again at 16.81 feet on Nov 1. On Nov 4, all ten members of Colorado's congressional delegation signed a letter backing the governor's Oct 31 request for a major disaster declaration. The letter says the flooding hit "approximately 700 homes across Archuleta and Rio Grande Counties, completely destroying 16 homes and 6 commercial buildings." The governor's $27 million estimate covers public infrastructure only: "This estimate does not include private property damage." Archuleta County opened a Disaster Assistance Center at 538 Village Drive, Pagosa Springs, from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily, Nov 5–11. The county warns that its damage survey "is NOT an application for disaster assistance." Hickenlooper, Bennet and Rep. Hurd toured flood damage in Guadalupe and South Fork on Nov 5.
*Why it matters:* there is still no request for FEMA help to individual households. Without that help, and later HUD disaster recovery grants, rebuilding about 700 homes depends on insurance, USDA repair loans and local relief funds. FEMA denied the state's request after the October 2025 floods in this same area. Rental and for-sale stock in Pagosa Springs and the San Luis Valley will be tight through the winter.
Sources: [Hickenlooper and Hurd delegation letter release](https://www.hickenlooper.senate.gov/press_releases/hickenlooper-hurd-lead-colorado-congressional-delegation-in-support-of-major-presidential-disaster-request-for-southwestern-colorado-flooding/) [Nov 4, 2026]; [Governor's Office update](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/governor-polis-provides-update-southwestern-colorado-flooding-state-response-and-voting-access) [Nov 3, 2026]; [Rocky Mountain PBS](https://www.rmpbs.org/news/science-environment/historic-back-to-back-floods-shock-pagosa-springs-residents) [Nov 3, 2026]; [Pagosa Springs Sun, county update](https://www.pagosasun.com/stories/county-issues-nov-6-flood-update,158973) [Nov 6, 2026]; [Hickenlooper tour release](https://www.hickenlooper.senate.gov/press_releases/hickenlooper-bennet-hurd-tour-flooding-in-san-luis-valley/) [Nov 5, 2026]

## Federal funding: HUD and Congress

**HUD's Express Lane for LIHTC construction loans.** Mortgagee Letter 2026-13 took effect when it was issued on Nov 2. It applies to FHA Section 221(d)(4) loans on new-construction 9% and 4% bond deals, with no loan size or unit cap. Deals with project-based Section 8 are excluded. To qualify, the team needs:
- **Developer:** at least three LIHTC deals "developed, placed in service, and stabilized" in the past five years, plus $1 million in liquidity and $5 million in net worth.
- **General contractor:** five 221(d)(4) and LIHTC jobs and five Davis-Bacon jobs in the past 10 years.
- **Architect:** five 221(d)(4) jobs in the past 10 years.
- **Manager:** at least five LIHTC properties operating now.

In return:
- HUD decides on a complete firm commitment application within 30 days.
- Required equity at initial endorsement drops to 10%.
- Final plans and third-party reports can come in as late as 15 days before closing.

*Why it matters:* for a Colorado 4% bond deal with an experienced team, this could take months off FHA construction financing. Smaller and rural developers won't meet the experience test.
Sources: [HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-13 (posted by Novogradac)](https://www.novoco.com/public-media/documents/hud-mortgagee-letter-2026-13-11022026.pdf) [Nov 2, 2026]; [Nixon Peabody summary](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/11/04/hud-fast-tracks-fha-financing-for-experienced-lihtc-developers) [Nov 4, 2026]

**Federal funding runs out Dec 11, and a full HUD bill this year looks unlikely.** NLIHC's Nov 2 memo says "it is unlikely that Congress will be able to reach an agreement on a final FY27 spending bill for HUD programs" this year. Congress returns **Nov 9** with about five weeks in session. Advocates want the next stopgap to:
- renew expiring Continuum of Care projects for 12 months without competition;
- permanently block OMB's proposed grant rule;

For the full-year FY27 bill, separate from the stopgap, NLIHC asks for at least $45.6 billion for vouchers (tenant-based rental assistance), which it says "would be enough to renew all current TBRA contracts."

Days of action: **Nov 10** on the OMB rule, and **Nov 18** on CoC funding.
*Why it matters:* another stopgap would hold HOME, CDBG and voucher money at current levels and push 2027 allocations to Colorado grantees later into the year. Colorado housing authorities should not plan on new voucher awards until a full-year bill passes.
Source: [NLIHC Memo to Members](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/Memo_Nov_2_26_4.pdf) [Nov 2, 2026]

## Rates and the market

**Mortgage rates: 30-year fixed at 7.18%.** Freddie Mac's weekly average fell from 7.24% the week before. That is the third weekly drop in a row. A year ago it was 6.22%. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.57%.
Source: [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) [Nov 5, 2026]; figures as reported by [AP via WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2026/11/05/average-us-30-year-mortgage-rate-falls-718-this-week-its-third-straight-decline/)

**Metro Denver: concessions on two out of three sales.** The Denver Metro Association of Realtors' October report, released Nov 4, says 65% of October sales involved seller concessions. The full report is for members only, so we could not confirm prices or listing counts. The Denver Post covered the same report on Nov 5.
*Why it matters:* when most sellers are paying buyers' costs, sale prices overstate what homes really sold for. Use net prices when you size for-sale workforce homes against the market.
Source: [DMAR market trends reports](https://www.dmarealtors.com/market-trends-reports) [Nov 4, 2026]
