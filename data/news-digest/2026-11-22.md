## State programs: CHFA, DOH, OEDIT

**CHFA's 2026 Round Two awards: 845 rental homes, almost all in metro Denver.** On Nov 16 CHFA announced federal 4% and state housing tax credit reservations for nine new construction developments, totaling 845 affordable rental homes. CHFA estimates more than $234 million in private equity. The award report, dated as of Nov 10, gives these numbers:
- **Credits:** $19,668,117 in federal 4% credit; $11.7 million in accelerated state credit (all nine got the $1.3 million maximum); $4.2 million in standard state credit; $774,000 in Transit-Oriented Communities credit.
- **Bonds:** $146,387,212 in private activity bonds.
- **Where:** Aurora, Boulder, Broomfield, Castle Rock, Denver, Grand Junction, Lakewood and Littleton. Grand Junction (62 homes) is the only award outside metro Denver.
- **Who it serves:** all 845 are rental homes for lower-income renters. By income limit: 141 homes at 30% of area median income, 58 at 40%, 251 at 50%, 323 at 60%, 54 at 70% and 18 at 80%. CHFA says the homes will serve families, older adults, veterans, youth leaving foster care and people coming out of homelessness.
- **Competition:** CHFA's applicant report lists 26 applications for 2,727 homes, asking for about $33.8 million in accelerated state credit. The only rural applicant, in Cortez, did not win.

*Why it matters:* the state credit is what makes 4% bond deals pencil, and this round it went almost entirely to the metro area. In 2023, 2024 and 2025, Round Two each had one or two winners outside metro Denver. If you're planning a rural 4% deal for 2027, expect to need another gap source, or to score differently than this year's rural applicant did. The 141 homes at 30% AMI are the units that reach the renters the state's needs assessment says are most cost-burdened.
Sources: [CHFA release](https://www.chfainfo.com/chfa-news/11162026-2026-round-two-htc-awards) [Nov 16, 2026]; [CHFA Round Two award report (PDF)](https://www.chfainfo.com/getContentAsset/8211a644-1911-4044-a07f-5847fe1e283c/141d77fc-2e06-49eb-b14c-2ff58f5ce730/2026-Round-Two-Housing-Tax-Credit-Award-Report.pdf?language=en-US) [as of Nov 10, 2026]; [CHFA Round Two applicant report (PDF)](https://www.chfainfo.com/getContentAsset/3adb001c-dcd8-48e6-bdd9-88bf191ee7cd/50c2bc4c-a8ff-46ba-95c2-0e74752d147f/2026-HTC-Round-Two-Applicant-Report.pdf?language=en-US); [CHFA awards page](https://www.chfainfo.com/rental-housing/housing-credit/awards) [checked Nov 22, 2026]; map and analysis: [COHO research brief](https://cohoanalytics.com/research-brief.html?id=chfa-2026-round-two-awards)

**CHFA Colorado Champions: home loans for first responders.** CHFA announced on Nov 20 a program that opened Nov 2 under Senate Bill 26-053. It raises CHFA's mortgage income limit to 110% of its usual guidelines for eligible buyers, which allows incomes up to $196,810. Buyers get a 30-year fixed loan plus down payment and closing cost help of up to $25,000 or 4% of the first mortgage, whichever is less. Eligible buyers include certified peace officers, deputy sheriffs, firefighters, EMTs, dispatchers, corrections officers, port of entry officers and wildlife officers. CHFA Executive Director Thomas Bryan said many first responders "face challenges affording a home where they work." It follows the school employee program CHFA opened earlier this year under SB25-167.
*Why it matters:* this is an owner program, and it reaches incomes well above where most for-sale affordable housing is priced. In resort and Front Range towns that recruit officers and firefighters, it can help close the gap on a market-rate starter home. It does nothing for renters.
Source: [CHFA release](https://www.chfainfo.com/chfa-news/11202026-chfa-colorado-champions) [Nov 20, 2026]

## Prop 123 and local commitments

**Colorado Springs met its three-year Prop 123 goal.** The city says DOLA confirmed it reached its 2024–2026 commitment of 2,274 qualifying affordable homes. It has filed a smaller 2027–2029 commitment of 1,278 homes. The city cites its 2025 housing needs assessment, which found a shortage of about 27,000 homes, and says it uses tax credits, state and federal funds, and city and utility fee rebates "without relying on the City's General Fund." Mayor Yemi Mobolade said, "We have made meaningful progress, but there is still more work to do."
*Why it matters:* the state's second-largest city is now in line for Prop 123 money in the next cycle. Its next goal is little more than half the last one, so developers there should expect the city to be choosier about which projects it counts.
Sources: [City of Colorado Springs](https://coloradosprings.gov/news/colorado-springs-meets-major-affordable-housing-goal-expands-housing-opportunities-residents) [Nov 19, 2026]; [KKTV](https://www.kktv.com/2026/11/19/colorado-springs-meets-major-housing-goal-with-millions-state-funding-supporting-new-developments/) [Nov 19, 2026]

## Local actions and ballot results

**Denver's affordable housing ordinance went before the 10th Circuit.** On Nov 16 a Denver homebuilder, represented by the Pacific Legal Foundation, argued that Denver's Mandatory Affordable Housing Ordinance (in effect since 2022) is an unconstitutional taking. The ordinance makes builders either price some homes below market or pay a linkage fee. A federal district judge dismissed the case in March. The builder's lawyer said four single-family homes would owe $25,000 in fees and two duplexes $45,000, and that "nobody building under 10 units will choose to do mandated affordable housing." The city didn't comment. In a June filing it argued the law is not an "uncompensated taking."
*Why it matters:* if the appeals court revives the claim, linkage fees and inclusionary rules across Colorado could face the same test. Many cities use those fees as local gap money for tax credit deals. No ruling date has been set.
Source: [Denver Gazette](https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/11/17/builder-sues-city-of-denver-over-affordable-housing-fees/) [Nov 17, 2026]

**Aurora is recruiting for a rental registration task force.** The city wants 13 volunteers, including neighborhood residents, property owners, nonprofit housing advocates and legal aid representatives, to help write a rental registration and licensing ordinance. City staffer Joshua King said the aim is to "get on top of violations quicker." Organizers in northwest Aurora described pests, mold and a gas leak that went unfixed for three months.
*Why it matters:* a licensing program would give Aurora a way to enforce conditions in older apartments, where many lower-income renters live. If you own rentals in Aurora, this is the time to weigh in on fees and inspection rules.
Sources: [Denver7](https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/in-your-community/aurora-arapahoe-county/aurora-recruits-volunteers-for-rental-task-force-as-advocates-push-for-stronger-tenant-protections) [Nov 2026]; Colorado Politics headline [Nov 18, 2026]

**Ballot measures: no new counts we could read.** COHO's ballot data still has no result for Broomfield 1A (a lodging tax for the housing authority) or Mt. Crested Butte 2A (a short-term rental tax for community housing). County canvasses finish by **Nov 25**.

## Disasters and housing loss

**Second flood disaster request asks for household aid in four counties.** This week Gov. Polis sent President Trump a second Major Disaster Declaration request, for the flooding that began Oct 29–31 in southwest Colorado and the San Luis Valley. The first request, for the Oct 22–25 storm, is still pending. The state had to file twice because the Weather Service treats the two storms as separate events. The new request asks for:
- **Individual Assistance** (money for households, case management, disaster unemployment and legal aid) in Archuleta, Conejos, La Plata and Rio Grande counties;
- **Public Assistance** for roads, bridges and public buildings in those four counties plus Mineral and Montezuma;
- **Hazard mitigation** funding statewide.

Joint assessments counted **271 primary homes destroyed or unlivable** from the second storm, another **136 damaged homes**, and $28.5 million in public damage. At the peak more than 6,300 people were under evacuation orders. The release says "Some households may remain without road access until spring." Polis said "Approving one without the other would cause confusion." This is the state's 13th declared disaster of the year.
*Why it matters:* last week's damage counts have turned into a formal request for household aid. Until FEMA approves it, rebuilding depends on insurance, SBA loans and local funds. With 271 homes gone, expect more pressure on winter rentals and motel rooms in Pagosa Springs, Durango and the San Luis Valley, and expect local housing authorities to look for emergency voucher help.
Sources: [Governor's Office release as printed by the Pagosa Springs Sun](https://www.pagosasun.com/stories/governor-submits-second-presidential-major-disaster-declaration-for-southwestern-colorado-flooding,160530) [Nov 20, 2026]; [Colorado Politics](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/20/polis-submits-second-major-disaster-declaration-application-for-floods/) [Nov 20, 2026]; [KKTV](https://www.kktv.com/2026/11/20/gov-jared-polis-requests-2nd-major-disaster-declaration-over-southwest-colorado-flooding/) [Nov 20, 2026]

## Federal funding: HUD and Congress

**Court blocks HUD's homelessness grant rewrite.** On Fri Nov 20, US District Judge Mary McElroy in Rhode Island ruled that HUD overstepped the McKinney-Vento Act in its FY2026 Continuum of Care competition. HUD had planned to set aside $1.3 billion of the more than $4 billion Congress provided for transitional housing and services-only grants, which would have moved money away from permanent housing. She ordered HUD to let applicants revise and resubmit through **Dec 1** and to award funds by **Dec 31**. Her ruling also struck a ban on harm-reduction work and a "self-sufficiency" test. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the ruling "disrupts $4 billion for our most vulnerable" and that HUD may appeal. The challenge came from 22 states, DC and advocacy groups.
*Why it matters:* Colorado's continuums of care fund permanent supportive housing through this program. The order keeps renewals on the old footing for now, but an appeal could reopen it. Applicants should be ready to resubmit by Dec 1.
Source: [Reuters, via KELO](https://kelo.com/2026/11/20/us-judge-rejects-trump-administrations-homelessness-program-revamp/) [Nov 20, 2026]

**HUD's mixed-status rule is final, effective Dec 14, and is being challenged in court.** HUD finalized the rule on Nov 12. It bars households with mixed immigration status from public housing, project-based Section 8, Housing Choice Vouchers and other programs, and it ends prorated assistance. Every member of an assisted household, including US citizens, will have to verify status. Owners can grant up to a 60-day extension to produce documents. NLIHC estimates about 80,000 people could be displaced nationwide, nearly half of them children. Tenants and advocacy groups in Los Angeles sued on Nov 16. Legal Aid of New York filed a complaint on Nov 19. NLIHC is asking Congress to block the rule in the next stopgap.
*Why it matters:* Colorado housing authorities and owners of project-based Section 8 buildings will have to re-verify every tenant. That costs staff time and may leave units empty while families sort out documents. Some Colorado voucher and public housing families will face the choice of splitting up or losing help.
Sources: [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/hud-finalizes-rule-mixed-immigration-status-households/875086/) [Nov 2026]; [NLIHC Memo to Members](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/Memo_Nov_16_26_1.pdf) [Nov 16, 2026]; [Legal Aid complaint, file-stamped Nov 19 (PDF)](https://legalaidnyc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/File-Stamp-11.19.26-1120-am-Mixed-Status-Families-Complaint.pdf)

**Senate passes the permitting bill, 62–33.** The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act on **Thu Nov 19 at about 7:30 p.m. MT**, using H.R. 2347 as the vehicle. Thirty-one Democrats and two Republicans voted no. Because the House passed an earlier version, it has to vote again, which is expected after Thanksgiving. Last week housing groups said the bill would drop federal historic review and speed environmental review for many HOME, CDBG and other HUD-funded projects. We couldn't confirm which housing provisions survived in the final text.
*Why it matters:* faster federal review would take months off Colorado deals that use HOME or CDBG money. Nothing changes until the House acts and the president signs it.
Sources: [Senate roll call vote 270](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_votes/vote1192/vote_119_2_00270.htm) [Nov 19, 2026]; [Roll Call](https://www.rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) [Nov 19, 2026]

**Funding runs out Dec 11, and there's still no Senate HUD bill.** NLIHC's Nov 16 memo says Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins released seven of the 12 remaining FY27 bills, including the one that funds USDA rural housing. She did not release the HUD bill. Advocates want the next stopgap to renew Continuum of Care projects expiring in FY26 for 12 months without a competition.
*Why it matters:* Colorado housing authorities and rural USDA borrowers should keep planning for flat funding and late allocations.
Source: [NLIHC Memo to Members](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/Memo_Nov_16_26_1.pdf) [Nov 16, 2026]

## Rates and the market

**Mortgage rates: 30-year fixed at 7.28%.** Freddie Mac's Nov 19 average rose from 7.11%, the first increase in five weeks. A year ago it was 6.26%. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.66%, up from 6.45%. These are rates for owners buying a home.
*Why it matters:* the small relief buyers saw in October and early November is gone. For-sale workforce projects should keep assuming slow sales into winter.
