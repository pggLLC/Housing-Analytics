## The short version

1. **The state's first housing needs assessment: one in three households is cost-burdened.** The figure is 49% of renters and 26% of owners. DOLA puts the need at about 30,600 new homes a year through 2036.
2. **The flood damage count now covers households.** Joint federal, state and county teams counted damage Nov 10–12, including for FEMA's Individual Assistance program. That program is the one that pays households directly. So far 311 displaced households have had emergency lodging.
3. **Forest Service land leases can now back a loan.** A final rule published Nov 2 takes administrative site leases out of the special-use rules that kept lenders away. It takes effect around Dec 2. Mountain counties with Forest Service sites in mind should take note.
4. **The Senate advanced a permitting bill that reaches HUD money, 73–25.** Housing groups say it would cut federal historic and environmental review on many HOME and CDBG projects. Hickenlooper voted to advance it and Bennet voted no. A final Senate vote is expected early this coming week.
5. **Rates fell to 7.11% and Colorado home sales fell nearly 19% from last October.** The statewide median single-family price was $575,000, nearly flat on the year, and homes took 72 days to sell.

## State programs: CHFA, DOH, OEDIT

**DOLA's first statewide housing needs assessment.** DOLA's State Demography Office released the report on Nov 9, as SB24-174 requires. The main numbers:
- **Cost burden:** about 34% of households (842,900) spend more than 30% of income on housing. That is 49% of renters and 26% of owners.
- **Need:** about 30,600 new homes a year through 2036, based on projected population and household growth.
- **Income needed:** $153,000 to buy the median home ($547,900), and $72,900 to rent the average apartment. The average two-bedroom rents for $1,932.
- **Vacancy and costs:** statewide rental vacancy is 9.5%. Construction costs rose 44% from 2019 to 2024, while single-family sale prices rose 37%.
- **Who's coming:** people 65 and older are projected to grow 19% over ten years, and people with a disability by up to 238,000.

DOLA Executive Director Maria De Cambra said "Behind every data point are families hoping for stability and an affordable place to put down roots."
*Why it matters:* this is now the state's own number for need. Expect it in 2027 bills, Prop 123 debates and funding applications. A 9.5% rental vacancy rate next to a 49% renter cost burden says the problem is price, not a lack of units. New supply has to reach lower AMI bands to change that burden figure. The release doesn't spell out what local governments have to do next under SB24-174.
Sources: [DOLA release](https://cdola.colorado.gov/press-release/dola-releases-first-ever-statewide-housing-needs-assessment) [Nov 9, 2026]; [report (DOLA download link)](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1T9pIRab6usCUyPfg7C3n-D9YSDTD3IS4)

## Local actions and ballot results

**Aurora: a former luxury townhome site becomes affordable housing.** The East Colfax Community Collective, a nonprofit, got a $2.8 million state affordable housing grant for 1310–1380 Yosemite Street in northwest Aurora. The 1.34-acre lot was once planned for townhomes starting at $600,000. State grant documents list 152 units. The group says it will "work closely with community members to define the most important needs for the area." Income levels, and whether the homes will be rentals or for sale, haven't been set.
*Why it matters:* it's an example of state money buying land along a corridor before prices push lower-income residents out. With no income targets yet, it's too early to count these as Prop 123 units.
Source: [Sentinel Colorado](https://sentinelcolorado.com/metro/colfax-group-gets-2-8m-grant-to-switch-aurora-luxury-home-project-into-affordable-housing/) [Nov 12, 2026]

**Denver: shelters are 96% full, and 8% of people leave for housing.** Figures from Denver's Department of Housing Stability show congregate shelters at 96% average occupancy. Stays average about 50 days. "Just 8% of people leaving those shelters have what the city considers a positive exit." The city has about 3,000 shelter beds, about 1,600 of them congregate. Overall homelessness rose from 4,794 in 2022 to about 6,411 in 2026, while unsheltered homelessness fell 64%.
*Why it matters:* shelters are full because there is nowhere to move people on to. More supportive housing and deeply targeted units would do more than more beds.
Source: [9News, via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/denvers-shelters-96-full-few-041405091.html) [Nov 11, 2026]

**State and FTC settle with a large single-family rental landlord.** Attorney General Phil Weiser's office and the FTC reached a $48.5 million settlement with an Arizona-based company that manages about 90,000 rental homes in 17 states. They alleged misleading advertised rents, hidden fees, homes rented out still needing repair, and refunds that were never paid. The company admits no wrongdoing.
*Why it matters:* Colorado keeps going after advertised-rent and junk-fee practices. If you manage rentals, the rent you advertise should be the total a tenant actually pays.
Source: [Colorado Politics](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/11/09/false-advertising-of-single-family-rentals-flagged-in-a-settlement/) [Nov 9, 2026]

## Disasters and housing loss

**Southwest Colorado floods: the damage count now includes households.** Federal, state and county teams ran joint preliminary damage assessments Nov 10–12. They covered Conejos, Rio Grande, Mineral, Archuleta, La Plata and Montezuma counties, and both FEMA programs: Public Assistance for public infrastructure and Individual Assistance for households. SBA and United Way 211 were on the teams. The state is still deciding whether to amend its first disaster request or file a second one for the Oct 31 storm. As of Nov 10:
- the four Disaster Assistance Centers had helped more than 1,000 people (Pagosa Springs closed Nov 11);
- 211 and a home-share partnership had lodged **311 displaced households**.

US 160 over Wolf Creek Pass opened for travel Nov 14–15 and closes again Mon Nov 16 at 6 a.m. for paving. Pagosa Springs Town Manager David Harris told KUNC the missing piece is "Just the disaster declaration. That's the big kahuna."
*Why it matters:* last week's request covered public infrastructure only. Now Individual Assistance is being counted, which is the first step toward FEMA money for households. Without it, about 700 damaged homes will be rebuilt with SBA loans, insurance, and local relief funds. Many owners had no flood insurance. Expect 311 households to compete for winter rentals in Pagosa Springs, Durango and the San Luis Valley.
Sources: [Governor's Office flood update](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/governor-polis-provides-update-flooding-southwestern-colorado-shares-resources-support) [Nov 10, 2026]; [KUNC/CPR](https://www.kunc.org/regional-news/2026-11-11/southern-coloradans-hit-by-floods-pray-for-federal-assistance) [Nov 11, 2026]; [Governor's Office, Wolf Creek Pass](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/us-160-wolf-creek-pass-open-weekend-travel-nov-14-and-15) [Nov 12, 2026]

## Federal funding: HUD and Congress

**Forest Service leasing rule is final: leases can now serve as loan collateral.** The final rule (RIN 0596-AD68) takes administrative site leases out of the special-use regulations. It was published Nov 2 and takes effect 30 days later, around **Dec 2**. Under the old framework, leases limited "the use of the leasehold interest as collateral for loans," limited "assignment and transfer of the lease," and allowed "unilateral termination of the authorization by the Forest Service." Without the change, the rule says, partners "will continue to face challenges in obtaining the financing needed to pursue affordable housing." Collateral, assignment, default and lender step-in will now be "governed by and subject to the lease terms." It creates no new leasing authority. Summit Daily and the Aspen Times reported on Nov 12 that mountain leaders call it a "gamechanger." Their site blocks our reader, so we could not confirm the quotes.
*Why it matters:* lenders and tax credit investors had trouble with leaseholds the Forest Service could end at will. Summit County's Dillon project ran into this problem. Mountain counties weighing Forest Service sites can now negotiate leases a lender will accept. Get the lender's and investor's counsel to read the lease form early.
Sources: [Federal Register public inspection copy, final rule 2026-22296](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22296.pdf) [Nov 2, 2026]; [Forest Service release as republished by Montana Outdoor](https://www.montanaoutdoor.com/2026/11/forest-service-finalizes-administrative-site-leasing-rule-to-reduce-regulatory-barriers-and-improve-leasing-flexibility/) [Nov 2, 2026]; [Summit Daily headline](https://www.summitdaily.com/news/usfs-rule-colorado-mountain-affordable-housing-projects/) [Nov 12, 2026]

**Senate advances the permitting bill, 73–25.** On Nov 9 the Senate voted 73–25 to move forward on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (S. 5653). Housing groups wrote to senators that it "would alleviate significant, major burdens in HUD programs":
- many HUD- and USDA-funded projects would no longer need federal historic preservation review, and would likely get only streamlined environmental review;
- the groups say about $10 billion a year in housing programs is affected, including CDBG, HOME and public housing;
- Clean Water Act reviews would be streamlined too.

The House has no companion bill yet.
*Why it matters:* for a Colorado deal with HOME or CDBG money, environmental and historic review often adds months. If this becomes law, that time could shrink. Nothing changes until it passes both chambers.
Sources: [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/baaja-permitting-bill-speed-apartment-construction-advances-in/874433/) [Nov 11, 2026]; [NACo](https://www.naco.org/news/us-senate-advances-bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act-initial-procedural-vote) [Nov 2026]

**Funding runs out Dec 11, and the next stopgap will probably run into 2027.** NLIHC's Nov 9 memo expects no final FY27 HUD bill this year. It expects the next stopgap to run into early 2027, leaving the new Congress to finish. Advocates want that stopgap to:
- renew Continuum of Care projects expiring in FY26 for 12 months without competition (NLIHC says HUD's proposed changes put "an estimated 97,000 people who previously experienced homelessness at risk of losing their housing");
- block OMB's proposed grant rule.

NLIHC also says advocates expect HUD to issue a final mixed-status rule, which would make families with members of different immigration status choose between splitting up and losing assistance.
*Why it matters:* Colorado housing authorities and CoCs should plan on flat funding and late allocations into spring. A mixed-status rule would hit some Colorado public housing and voucher households. Watch for it in the Federal Register.
Source: [NLIHC Memo to Members](https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/Memo_Nov_9_26_3.pdf) [Nov 9, 2026]

## Rates and the market

**Mortgage rates: 30-year fixed at 7.11%.** Freddie Mac's Nov 12 average fell from 7.18%, the fourth weekly drop in a row. A year ago it was 6.24%. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.45%.
Source: [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) [Nov 12, 2026]

**Colorado home sales in October: down nearly 19%, prices flat.** The Colorado Association of Realtors' October snapshot shows:
- **Sales:** 6,079 closed sales statewide, down 18.6% from last October.
- **Pending sales:** 5,700 homes went under contract, down 17.2% from 6,883, so the slowdown carries into the next few months.
- **New listings:** 8,833, down 8.9%.
- **Prices:** the median single-family price was $575,000 (down 0.7%). Condos and townhomes were $415,000 (about flat).
- **Speed:** homes took 72 days on average to sell, up from 69, and sellers got 97.9% of list price.

*Why it matters:* prices are holding because new listings are falling too, so this isn't a price correction. Workforce for-sale projects should expect slower absorption and plan for longer marketing periods, not lower prices.
Source: [CAR October 2026 snapshot (PDF)](https://coloradorealtors.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CARInfoFB_Oct2026.pdf) [Nov 12, 2026]; [CAR market trends page](https://coloradorealtors.com/market-trends/)
